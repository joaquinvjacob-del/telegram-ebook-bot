Introduction

Welcome — you now have everything you need to read almost any classic book, completely free, for as long as you want.

In the next few minutes, you're going to:

  • Install one simple app (if you don't already have it)
  • Activate your personal access with the code you received
  • Search for and download your first book

No subscriptions. No monthly fees. No limit on how many books you download. Once you're set up, getting a new book takes about 15 seconds — less time than it takes to find your credit card.

What this actually is

Behind the scenes, this gives you access to a private tool that searches three of the largest legal libraries of free books in the world — Standard Ebooks, Project Gutenberg, and Internet Archive — all at once, and hands you the file instantly, already picked from the best-quality source available. No hunting through five different websites, no sketchy pop-ups, no fake "download" buttons that install something you didn't ask for.

What this is not

To be completely clear: every single book you'll download through this is legally free and public domain — meaning the copyright has expired and nobody owns it anymore. This is not piracy, and it's not a gray area. You're not "getting away with" anything. These are libraries doing exactly what libraries are for.

Let's get you set up.

Part One

Installing Telegram

[Section image placeholder — Higgsfield prompt #2 in estructura-ebook.md]

Your access runs inside an app called Telegram — a free, extremely popular messaging app (similar to WhatsApp), available on every phone, tablet, and computer. If you already use Telegram, skip ahead to Part Two.

On iPhone / iPad

  1. Open the App Store.
  2. Tap the search icon and type Telegram.
  3. Tap Get, then confirm with Face ID, Touch ID, or your Apple ID password.
  4. Once it's installed, tap Open.

On Android

  1. Open the Play Store.
  2. Search for Telegram.
  3. Tap Install.
  4. Once it's installed, tap Open.

On a computer (Windows / Mac)

  1. Go to telegram.org in your browser.
  2. Click Get Telegram, and download the version for your operating system.
  3. Install it like you would any other program, then open it.

(You can also just use Telegram directly in your browser at web.telegram.org — no installation needed — though the app is more reliable for receiving files.)

Creating your account (first-time Telegram users only)

When you open Telegram for the first time, it will ask for your phone number to create your account. This is completely normal — it's how Telegram verifies you're a real person, the same as any messaging app. It will text you a short verification code; type that code in, and you're in. This takes under a minute.

Part Four

Getting Your Book Onto Your Device

[Section image placeholder — Higgsfield prompt #4 in estructura-ebook.md]

This is the part most guides skip — and it's exactly where most people get stuck. Here's exactly what to do, device by device.

📱 iPhone / iPad

  1. After tapping the download button, the file appears as a document inside your Telegram chat.
  2. Tap the file, then tap the share icon (the square with an arrow pointing up).
  3. Choose Save to Files, or if you have a reading app installed, choose Copy to [App Name] directly.
  4. If you saved it to Files, open the Books app (built into every iPhone) — it will usually pick it up automatically, or tap + inside Books and select the file.

🤖 Android

  1. Tap the file inside your Telegram chat, then tap the download icon to save it to your phone.
  2. Open whichever reading app you prefer (Google Play Books, Moon+ Reader, or similar — most are free).
  3. In the app, look for an Import, Add file, or + button, and select the file from your Downloads folder.

📖 Kindle — the most common sticking point

Kindle devices don't read EPUB files directly, so make sure you tap the Kindle format button (not EPUB) when you download. Once you have that file, you have three options:

Option A — Send to Kindle app (easiest, if available in your country):

  1. Save the file from Telegram to your phone (see iPhone/Android steps above).
  2. Open the free Send to Kindle app, sign in with your Amazon account, and select the file to send it straight to your Kindle library.

Option B — Email it to your Kindle:

  1. Every Kindle has its own email address, like yourname_123@kindle.com. Find yours at amazon.com → Manage Your Content and Devices → Preferences → Personal Document Settings.
  2. Save the file to your phone, then email it as an attachment to that address.
  3. It'll appear on your Kindle next time it connects to Wi-Fi (usually within a couple minutes).

Option C — USB cable (works 100% of the time, no internet needed):

  1. Save the file to your computer.
  2. Plug your Kindle into your computer with a USB cable.
  3. Your Kindle will appear as a drive — drag the file into the documents folder.
  4. Unplug, and it'll show up on your Kindle's home screen.

📗 Kobo and other e-readers

Kobo reads EPUB natively:

  1. Save the EPUB file to your computer.
  2. Connect your Kobo via USB — it appears as a drive.
  3. Drag the file into the main folder, then safely eject.

For any other e-reader brand, the USB method above almost always works the same way, since EPUB is the universal standard format.
