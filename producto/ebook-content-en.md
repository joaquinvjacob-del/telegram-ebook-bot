## Part 1 — Installing Telegram

Your access runs inside an app called **Telegram** — a free, extremely popular messaging app (similar to WhatsApp), available on every phone, tablet, and computer. If you already use Telegram, skip ahead to Part 2.

### On iPhone / iPad
1. Open the **App Store**.
2. Tap the search icon and type **Telegram**.
3. Tap **Get**, then confirm with Face ID, Touch ID, or your Apple ID password.
4. Once it's installed, tap **Open**.

### On Android
1. Open the **Play Store**.
2. Search for **Telegram**.
3. Tap **Install**.
4. Once it's installed, tap **Open**.

### On a computer (Windows / Mac)
1. Go to **telegram.org** in your browser.
2. Click **Get Telegram**, and download the version for your operating system.
3. Install it like you would any other program, then open it.

*(You can also just use Telegram directly in your browser at web.telegram.org — no installation needed — though the app is more reliable for receiving files.)*

### Creating your account (first-time Telegram users only)

When you open Telegram for the first time, it will ask for your phone number to create your account. This is completely normal — it's how Telegram verifies you're a real person, the same as any messaging app. It will text you a short verification code; type that code in, and you're in. This takes under a minute.

- **"This code has already been used on another account"** means that exact code was already activated somewhere — usually because it was copy-pasted with an extra space or character the first time and technically "failed" without you realizing it actually went through. ## Part 3 — How to Use the Bot

This is the easy part. There's no command to remember — you just type.

### Searching for a book

Simply type the title (or the author's name) directly into the chat, like you're texting a friend:

```
Pride and Prejudice
```

Send it, and within a couple of seconds you'll see a numbered list of matching books.

**💡 Tip:** If a book doesn't show up under its exact title, try searching just the author's last name instead — e.g., "Dickens" instead of "A Tale of Two Cities." This works especially well for books with long or unusual subtitles.

### Picking your book

Under the list, you'll see number buttons (1, 2, 3...). Tap the number next to the book you want.

If there are more results than fit on one screen, you'll see navigation arrows (⏮ ◀️ ▶️ ⏭) — tap them to move between pages without losing your place.

### Choosing a format

Once you pick a book, you'll see buttons for the formats available — usually:
- **EPUB** — works on almost everything except Kindle (phones, tablets, Kobo, most reading apps)
- **Kindle** — built specifically for Amazon Kindle devices and apps
- **PDF** — a fixed-layout version, works everywhere but doesn't resize text as nicely on small screens

Tap the format that matches your device (see Part 4 for exactly what to do with it once you have it), and the file lands directly in your chat within a few seconds.

### Quick access to popular classics

If you don't have a specific title in mind, send:

```
/popular
```

This instantly shows you 10 of the most-requested classics of all time — no searching required.

### Starting fresh

If your chat ever gets cluttered, send `/reset` and the bot will clean up the conversation and show you the welcome message again. Nothing about your access is affected — it's purely a "tidy up the chat" button.

## Part 4 — Getting Your Book Onto Your Specific Device

This is the part most guides skip — and it's exactly where most people get stuck. Here's exactly what to do, device by device.

### 📱 iPhone / iPad

1. After tapping the download button in the bot, the file appears as a document inside your Telegram chat.
2. Tap the file, then tap the **share icon** (the square with an arrow pointing up).
3. Choose **Save to Files**, or if you have a reading app installed (like Apple Books, Kindle app, or Kobo app), choose **Copy to [App Name]** directly from that share menu.
4. If you saved it to Files, open the **Books** app (built into every iPhone) — it will usually pick it up automatically, or you can tap **+** inside Books and select the file from Files.

### 🤖 Android

1. Tap the file inside your Telegram chat, then tap the **download icon** to save it to your phone.
2. Open whichever reading app you prefer (Google Play Books, Moon+ Reader, or similar — most are free on the Play Store).
3. In the app, look for an **Import**, **Add file**, or **+** button, and select the file from your **Downloads** folder.

### 📖 Kindle — the most common sticking point

Kindle devices don't read EPUB files directly, so make sure you tap the **Kindle format button** in the bot (not EPUB) when you download. Once you have that file, you have three options:

**Option A — Send to Kindle app (easiest, if available in your country):**
1. Save the file from Telegram to your phone (see iPhone/Android steps above).
2. Open the free **Send to Kindle** app (search for it in your app store), sign in with your Amazon account, and select the file to send it straight to your Kindle library.

**Option B — Email it to your Kindle:**
1. Every Kindle has its own email address, which looks like `yourname_123@kindle.com`. Find yours at **amazon.com → Manage Your Content and Devices → Preferences → Personal Document Settings**.
2. Save the file to your phone, then email it as an attachment to that address from any email app.
3. It'll appear on your Kindle next time it connects to Wi-Fi (usually within a couple of minutes).

**Option C — USB cable (works 100% of the time, no internet needed):**
1. Save the file to your computer.
2. Plug your Kindle into your computer with a USB cable.
3. Your Kindle will appear as a drive — drag the file into the **documents** folder.
4. Unplug, and it'll show up on your Kindle's home screen.

### 📗 Kobo and other e-readers

Kobo reads EPUB natively, so:
1. Save the EPUB file to your computer.
2. Connect your Kobo via USB — it appears as a drive.
3. Drag the file into the main folder, then safely eject.

For any other e-reader brand, the USB method above almost always works the same way, since EPUB is the universal standard format.
